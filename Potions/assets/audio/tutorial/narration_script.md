# Tutorial Narration Script

These are the exact body text strings used to generate the MP3 files. Slide titles are intentionally not narrated.

01. `tutorial_01_mission.mp3`
You manage a small R&D lab. Chemists can collaborate to discover new chemical products that your company can sell. First, you will learn what chemists actually do during a round.

02. `tutorial_02_first_team.mp3`
Drag the two chemists close together. When they are close enough, they form a team and can learn from each other's results after each round.

03. `tutorial_03_known_compounds.mp3`
Right now both chemists know the same six starting compounds. In each round, each chemist independently chooses a small set of compounds to test. Some combinations create new products; many do not.

04. `tutorial_04_try_round.mp3`
To have each chemist test a combination and then learn from their team, press the Next round button.

05. `tutorial_05_try_again.mp3`
Press Next round again. Each chemist will independently test another combination that could create a new product.

06. `tutorial_06_discovery_core.mp3`
A chemist tested a combination and created a new product. In the real task, you decide how many chemists to use and how to arrange them between rounds.

07. `tutorial_07_innovation_tracks.mp3`
The tracker shows two product tracks, A and B. Compounds farther to the right are more valuable. The final crossover product can only be found when at least one chemist has access to Tier 3 knowledge from both product tracks. Chemists do not know the hidden recipes; they choose from compounds they know, with higher-value compounds more likely to be selected.

08. `tutorial_08_funding.mp3`
Company funds show how much money the company has available. You can keep playing while company funds stay above zero. If the company runs out of money before the final crossover product is found, the game ends.

09. `tutorial_09_payroll.mp3`
Payroll is the company's cost for keeping chemists active. Each time a round is completed, payroll is deducted from company funds. Hiring more chemists increases payroll; firing chemists lowers payroll.

10. `tutorial_10_earnings.mp3`
Your earnings are what you receive as the participant. You earn bonuses only when the company discovers a new product for the first time: 50 cents for Tier 1 products, 1 dollar and 10 cents for Tier 2 products, 1 dollar and 90 cents for Tier 3 products, and 3 dollars and 60 cents for the final crossover product. Rediscoveries do not add to your earnings.

11. `tutorial_11_staffing.mp3`
Click the plus button to hire one chemist. Then click the minus button on any chemist to fire one. This teaches how payroll can grow or shrink.

12. `tutorial_12_trio.mp3`
First hire one more chemist, then drag all three close together. Larger teams let more chemists observe and learn from one another's results.

13. `tutorial_13_team_discovery.mp3`
Press Next round. Each chemist will test their own combination, and the whole team can learn from any new product discovered by a teammate.

14. `tutorial_14_teams.mp3`
Now hire two more chemists, then arrange the practice lab into one pair and one trio. This is the kind of reconfiguration you will use during the real task.

15. `tutorial_15_reassigning.mp3`
Between rounds, you may keep teams as they are or change them. To practice this control, move one chemist from one current team into the other team while still ending with one pair and one trio.

16. `tutorial_16_discovery_payoff.mp3`
First-time discoveries fill the tracker and add revenue to company funds. Rediscovering something the company already found may change who knows it, but it does not pay again.

17. `tutorial_17_begin.mp3`
You will start fresh with a company budget and a small team of chemists. Arrange teams or leave chemists working alone, hire or fire chemists when you think it helps, and decide when to continue rounds. The final crossover product requires Tier 3 knowledge from both product tracks to come together. Your earnings come from first-time product discoveries: 50 cents for Tier 1, 1 dollar and 10 cents for Tier 2, 1 dollar and 90 cents for Tier 3, and 3 dollars and 60 cents for the final crossover product.

18. `tutorial_dynamic_discovery_found.mp3`
The team created a new product, and both chemists now know it. The company earns revenue for first-time discoveries, and that new knowledge can affect what chemists try in later rounds. Hit Next to continue.

19. `tutorial_dynamic_test_combination.mp3`
The bubble shows the combination this chemist selected and tested from the compounds they currently know. Some combinations create new products, but many do not. Hit Next to see what happened.

20. `tutorial_dynamic_no_product.mp3`
This combination did not create a new product. Many rounds will not produce a discovery, so you may need to keep trying or reorganize later. Hit Next to continue.

21. `tutorial_dynamic_independent_tests.mp3`
This bubble shows one chemist's tested combination. Notice that the chemist included a recently discovered compound. Chemists tend to prefer newer, higher-value compounds, so they often reuse recently discovered compounds instead of only using the starting compounds. Hit Next to see what happened.

22. `tutorial_dynamic_team_learns.mp3`
This chemist discovered a new product. Because all three chemists are on the same team, the other team members learn that new product too. Hit Next to continue.

23. `tutorial_dynamic_transfer_test.mp3`
No new product will be found in this practice round. Watch how chemists test combinations after you changed team membership. Hit Next to see the result.

24. `tutorial_dynamic_transfer_result.mp3`
No new product was found, but chemists on the same team now share access to knowledge held by their teammates. Moving chemists between teams can change who has access to which discoveries. Hit Next to continue.
